What is a perforated sheet?

A perforated sheet is a metal plate or sheet we punch with a repeating pattern of holes — round, square, hex, slotted, or as-per-drawing. Buyers also call it a sheet with holes, metal sheet with holes, hole sheet, punched sheet, punching sheet, or perforated plate. We manufacture it in mild steel (MS), galvanized iron (GI), stainless steel (SS 304 / 316), and aluminium / aluminum, cut to size with custom hole, pitch, open area, and blank margins.

On our floor the process is CNC / turret punching (or dedicated tooling for volume). Hole diameter, center-to-center pitch, arrangement (60° stagger or 90° straight), thickness, and the unperforated border all sit on the same drawing. That is how we quote — not from a vague —jali sheet— WhatsApp photo alone.

Jali vs wire mesh — please read this once

In India, jali / jaali often means either a punched perforated sheet or woven / welded wire mesh. What we sell on this page is a punched perforated sheet — solid metal with holes punched through it. It is not woven wire mesh, welded mesh, expanded metal, chain link, or mosquito net. If you need wire mesh, say so; we will route you correctly instead of punching the wrong product.

Also called perforated jali, punching jali, or chhed wali sheet when the intent is punched metal. Say the hole size and material in the same sentence and we both stay aligned.

Materials we punch — MS, GI, SS & aluminium

At GARG INDUSTRIAL MESH we run the same pattern language across four metals. Pick the metal for the job; we punch the holes to your drawing.

Mild steel (MS) perforated sheet

MS perforated sheet (mild steel perforated sheet, MS sheet with holes, MS punching sheet) is the workhorse for machine guards, indoor screens, fabrication blanks, and cost-sensitive industrial covers. We punch black / CRCA-style mild steel where specified. Finish options after punching typically start at mill / oil; powder coat or paint is common once the sheet leaves us. Outdoor life without coating is limited — for wet or coastal exposure we usually steer you to GI or SS.

Typical RFQ language: MS perforated sheet 2 mm — 4×8, round Ø8 / pitch 11, 60°, blank edge 25 mm. Also searched as MS jali sheet — we still mean punched plate, not woven mesh.

Galvanized iron (GI) perforated sheet

GI perforated sheet (galvanized iron / galvanised perforated sheet, G.I. sheet with holes, zinc-coated perforated sheet) is our default nudge for outdoor machine covers, cable-tray covers, plant screens, and HVAC panels that need zinc protection without jumping to stainless. We can punch pre-galvanized sheet or discuss post-galvanizing for heavy plate — say which route your drawing assumes, because cut edges and punch holes behave differently.

Spelling on RFQs does not matter to us: galvanized, galvanised, GI, or G.I. Local buyers also say jasti chidrit sheet. Spec thickness, hole, and whether you need powder coat over zinc.

Stainless steel perforated sheet — SS 304 & SS 316

Stainless steel perforated sheet (SS perforated sheet / plate, perforated stainless, SS jali in punched context) is what we recommend for food, pharma, chemical, marine-adjacent, and wet process work. We separate grades on purpose:

  • SS 304 perforated sheet (AISI 304 — UNS S30400 — EN 1.4301 — SUS / Inox) — general corrosion resistance, kitchens, architectural interiors, many filters and strainers. Also called 304 stainless perforated sheet.
  • SS 316 perforated sheet (AISI 316 — UNS S31600 — EN 1.4401) — molybdenum-bearing; preferred for chlorides, coastal / marine splash, chemical strainers, and aggressive washdown. Do not treat 316 as a footnote on a 304 quote — if the drawing says 316, we punch 316.

On request we discuss 304L / 316L, surface finish (2B, BA, No.4), and food-grade / filter-screen callouts. Price is by spec (thickness, hole field, qty) — per kg or per sq ft depending how your purchasing wants the line — not a fake fixed catalogue rate.

Aluminium / aluminum perforated sheet

Aluminium perforated sheet (aluminum perforated sheet, perforated aluminium panel, alu / ALU sheet with holes) is light, formable, and the metal architects reach for on facade cladding, sunscreens / brise soleil, false ceilings, and speaker grilles. We lead India copy with aluminium and cover aluminum for export RFQs. Common alloys on enquiry: 1100, 3003, 5052; finishes include mill, anodized, powder coated, and PVDF / RAL colour systems for facade panels.

Send overall panel size, hole / pitch or OA% target, blank margins for framing, and coating system. Custom as-per-drawing is normal for us — facade panels rarely match a single stock SKU.

Quick metal picker

Budget indoor / guards → MS. Outdoor without stainless → GI. Wet / food / chemical → SS 304 or 316. Facade, ceiling, light panels → aluminium. Copper and brass stay on their own buying guides — we do not mix those namespaces into this ferrous / ALU pillar.

Specs that belong on every RFQ

We quote from crawlable numbers, not adjectives. Put thickness, hole geometry, open area, sheet size, and blank edge in the same message. Specs live in HTML on our site for the same reason — so engineers and Google both find hole — pitch — OA%.

Thickness (gauge)

Thickness is the base metal before punching — mm or gauge. Common commercial asks we see: 0.5 mm, 0.8 mm, 1 mm, 1.2 mm, 1.5 mm, 2 mm, 3 mm, and heavier plate for guards and strainers. Rule of thumb on our press: hole size should be ≥ sheet thickness unless we confirm tooling and process for a tighter combo. Thicker gauge + tiny hole = higher risk of tool wear, burr, and distortion. Say if flatness is critical (facade / ceiling) so we plan leveling.

Blank edge / unperforated border — 0–100 mm

The blank edge (blank margin, solid border, unperforated border, side / end margin) is the strip we leave without holes — for clamps, welds, hems, gaskets, and frame land. We customize 0–100 mm every day. Beyond 100 mm is available on request. Write millimetres per side if they differ (e.g. LE 15 / RE 15 / TE 40 / BE 40). “20 mm border” means all four sides equal unless you say otherwise. Full perf to the edge (0 mm) is possible when process allows — expect breakout / partial holes at the trim line unless you forbid them in writing.

Open area (OA%) formulas

Open area — also called OA%, free area, or percent open — is the void percentage of the perforated field. It drives airflow, visibility, weight, coating cost, and acoustic face behaviour. We publish the standard engineering forms so your calc and our quote use the same language. D = hole diameter (or square side), P = pitch (centers).

Open area (OA%) formulas for round 60°, round 90°, square and hex perforated patterns
Pattern Open area formula
Round, 60° staggered OA% = (D2 × 90.69) / P2
Equivalent form: OA% = (π / (2√3)) × (D / P)2 × 100
Round, 90° straight (inline) OA% = (D2 × 78.54) / P2
Equivalent form: OA% = (π / 4) × (D / P)2 × 100
Square, straight or staggered OA% = (side2 / pitch2) × 100
Hex (honeycomb) Use our labelled pattern OA% for the H / P you pick, or send target OA% and we match a hex tooling set. Hex often lands in the high-OA architectural band (~60–75%+ on common patterns).
Spec tip

OA% is calculated on the punched field. Wide blank edges are solid metal — they raise panel weight even when field OA% is high. Example workhorse: Round H8 — P11 — 60° — OA ≈ 48%. If you only know “about 40% open for ventilation,” say the band and end use; we will propose hole / pitch.

What is a perforated sheet / sheet with holes?

A perforated sheet is a metal plate punched with a repeating hole pattern — also called a sheet with holes, punching sheet, or chhed wali sheet. We manufacture perforated metal sheets in MS, GI, SS and aluminium with specified hole size, pitch, open area and blank margins. It is not woven wire mesh.

What is the difference between MS, GI, SS and aluminium perforated sheets?

MS (mild steel) is economical for indoor guards and filters; paint or coat it for outdoor use. GI (galvanized) adds zinc for outdoor life and machine covers. SS 304/316 suits food, pharma, chemical and coastal work. Aluminium (aluminum) is light for facades, ceilings and grilles. We punch all four metals to your pattern from our Noida works.

Round vs square vs hex — which hole shape should I choose?

Round holes (often 60° stagger) are the most common for strength, airflow and filters. Square holes give a modern look and high open area for screens. Hex patterns maximize open area for ventilation and decorative panels. Share your use case and we will recommend hole, pitch and arrangement.

What is open area % and why does it matter?

Open area (OA%) is the percentage of the sheet that is holes. Higher OA% means more airflow, light and visibility but less remaining metal strength. We label patterns with hole size, pitch and OA% so you can match ventilation, filtration or facade targets before ordering.

Can you supply custom hole size and pitch?

What sheet sizes and thicknesses do you punch?

We punch common sheet sizes such as 4×8 ft (about 1220×2440 mm) and cut-to-size panels across practical industrial thicknesses for MS, GI, SS and aluminium. Rule of thumb: hole size should be at least equal to sheet thickness for clean punch quality. Tell us metal, thickness, hole and pitch when you RFQ.

Is perforated sheet the same as wire mesh or jali?

No. Perforated sheet is solid plate with punched holes. Wire mesh / woven jali is interlaced wires. In India, buyers often say MS jali or sheet with holes when they mean punched perforated sheet — we supply punched perforated metal, not woven mesh, unless you specifically ask for mesh.
