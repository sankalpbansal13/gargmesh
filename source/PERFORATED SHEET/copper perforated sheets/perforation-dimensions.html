Geometry

Hole Size, Pitch and Open Area on Copper Sheet

This is the part that makes or breaks airflow, strength, and looks. Get hole and pitch right. State orientation. Calculate open area — do not guess it from a photo. We quote custom hole sizes, pitches, open areas, and orientations on order at gargindustrialmesh.com.

Core terms

Term Symbol (common) Definition
Hole size w or d Diameter for round; side length for square; across-flats or across-points — say which — for hex
Pitch t or p Center-to-center distance between adjacent holes
Bridge (bar) c Metal left between holes: typically c = t − w on that pitch line
Open area A₀ / OA% % of area that is hole within the perforated field (blank edges excluded)
Orientation 60° / 90° How hole rows stagger relative to each other

Pattern families GARG quotes every day

Round 60° — staggered (DIN-style Rv) Round 90° — straight / square pitch Square 90° — straight rows Hex 60° — staggered hexagonal openings

If your print shows a hybrid or decorative pattern, send CAD/PDF. We tool or program to print when thickness and hole size allow.

60° vs 90° — why it matters

60° staggered packs holes more efficiently. Higher open area for the same hole and center distance than a 90° layout. Looks denser. Common default for screens and airflow.

90° (straight rows / square pitch) lines up in orthogonal rows. Easier visual alignment with rectangular frames and some architectural grids. Lower OA% than 60° at the same hole and pitch.

Open area formulas

Relative open area for the perforated field (industry / DIN-style expressions). These describe the punched zone only — not the finished panel with blank borders.

Pattern Formula Notes
Round 60° OA% = (w² × 90.69) / t² w = hole Ø, t = pitch. Industry standard for 60° stagger (≈90.7 if rounded).
Round 90° OA% = (w² × 78.54) / t² Equal to πw²/(4t²)×100 on a square (orthogonal) lattice.
Square 90° OA% ≈ 100 × (w / t)² w = side length; t = center pitch.
Hex 60° Depends on AF vs AP definition + pitch State across-flats or across-points. Send drawing if OA% is contractual.

Example: round 60°, w = 3.0 mm, t = 5.0 mm → OA% = (3² × 90.69) / 5² ≈ 32.6%. These formulas describe the perforated field only — blank margins reduce overall open fraction on the finished panel.

What OA% means: free area for air, light, or particles through the perforated field. It is not “how open the whole sheet looks from the loading dock” once you add fat blank edges. Say whether your calc uses field OA% or overall panel free area.

Spec tip

Quote either (hole + pitch + orientation) or (hole + target OA% + orientation) and let us propose pitch. Quoting all three as hard conflicts (“Ø5, pitch 6, OA 50%”) forces a physics argument. Pick the controlling requirements.

Square and hex notes

  • Square 90° — sharp corners and higher OA% for a given pitch ratio than round 90°. Corners concentrate stress; bridge width still matters. State side length and center pitch clearly.
  • Hex 60° — honeycomb read, efficient packing. Always mark AF (across flats) or AP (across points). Mixing the two on a PO is how you get the wrong opening.

Relative scale — hole, pitch, thickness

Relationship Practical meaning
Hole ≥ thickness Production-friendly punch floor. See perforated copper sheet thickness guide.
Pitch > hole Required — otherwise holes merge. Bridge c = t − w must stay workable.
High w/t ratio High OA%, thin bridges, more distortion risk — especially soft thin copper.
Low w/t ratio Stronger sheet, lower airflow / light transmission.

Scrap, cost, and open area

Higher OA% means more punched scrap and usually more press work per square metre of finished panel. Very high open area also weakens the sheet and can raise leveling / flatness cost. Mid-range OA% with a sensible pitch often beats “maximum holes” on installed cost. Blank edges add solid copper weight even when the field is open — account for them in shipping and price.

Bridge width — do not starve the metal

Ultra-narrow bridges raise tear and distortion risk. If you need extreme open area, expect a frank quote conversation on flatness, temper, and thickness.

Naming that clears a quote desk

  • Round 60° Ø5.0 / 8.0 (DIN-style: Rv 5–8)
  • Round 90° Ø3.0 / 5.0
  • Square 90° 4.0 × 4.0 / pitch 6.0
  • Hex 60° 6.0 AF / pitch 8.0
Don't order without…

Orientation. “3 mm holes on 5 mm pitch” is incomplete. 60° and 90° are different products. Also state whether pitch is center-to-center (standard) or edge-to-edge (rare — and confusing).
