Material selection

C11000 Electrolytic Copper Grades for Sheets with Holes

If your PO says “copper perforated sheet” and nothing else, you have not specified a metal. You have specified a colour. Chemistry drives conductivity, joining behaviour, hydrogen risk, cost, and lead time. Spec the UNS grade. Then GARG INDUSTRIAL MESH can buy the right blank and punch it.

For sheet, strip, and plate, the usual product standard is ASTM B152 / B152M. It covers chemical composition, temper, mechanical properties, and (where specified) electrical resistivity for the unperforated mill product. Punching is secondary manufacturing. Mill certificates typically cover the blank, not finished hole geometry. See ASTM B152 copper sheet standards.

Don't order without…

A UNS grade (C11000 / C12200 / C10200 / C10100 — not “pure copper” or “OFHC” alone), plus one sentence on end use: electrical / architectural / braze-heavy HVAC / vacuum. End use steers grade faster than a brochure photo. If the PO does not name a UNS number, ASTM B152 allows the supplier to furnish any copper listed in the spec — that is how you get surprises.

Why “just copper” is wrong

Commercial copper is not one alloy. Residual oxygen, phosphorus, and purity targets change what happens when you heat it, braze it, put it in vacuum, or ask it to carry current. Write the UNS number.

  • C11000 (ETP) — oxygen-bearing. Annealed conductivity in the ~100–101% IACS class. Hydrogen embrittlement risk when heated in reducing / hydrogen-bearing atmospheres.
  • C12200 (DHP) — phosphorus deoxidized (P 0.015–0.040%). CDA lists ~85% IACS. Better welding/brazing suitability than ETP for many processes.
  • C10200 (OF) / C10100 (OFE) — oxygen-free grades with tabulated oxygen maxima. High conductivity. Favoured where ETP’s Cu₂O hydrogen-embrittlement mechanism is a problem. “OFHC” is a trade name, not a single UNS — buyers often mean C10200, sometimes C10100. Write the number.

Spec tip

Write both grade and product standard: ASTM B152 C11000. Prefer UNS numbers over “ETP,” “DHP,” or “OFHC” alone. If you only have a local name (T2, TP2, Cu-DHP), ask GARG to confirm the nearest ASTM equivalent before production.

C11000 — default perforated copper

Most architectural screens, general industrial panels, and a large share of conductivity-driven work land on C11000. High conductivity (min 100% IACS annealed; often listed ~101%). Soft temper forms well. Oxygen is present — not a defect, a chemistry fact.

Hydrogen embrittlement is the joining differentiator, not “copper cannot be joined.” Cuprous oxide plus hydrogen in a reducing atmosphere can leave steam at grain boundaries. That risk shows up in heating / welding / braze cycles — not in room-temperature punching. CDA rates oxyacetylene welding Not Recommended and gas-shielded arc Fair for C11000; soldering is Excellent, brazing Good. Do not overstate “cannot be welded” — process matters. If reducing heat is in the plan, talk before you lock ETP.

C12200 — when joining beats peak conductivity

Phosphorus at 0.015–0.040% kills free oxygen. CDA joining ratings are stronger across the board than ETP for welding processes. You pay with ~85% IACS (CDA tabulated). For heat-exchanger style work and torch/furnace joining after perforation, that trade is usually correct. For max-conductivity EMI or bus-related shields, it is usually wrong unless someone already accepted the hit in the calc.

C10200 OF and C10100 OFE — not “OFHC” as a grade

OFHC (oxygen-free high conductivity) is a historical/trade label. It is not one UNS number. On drawings and POs, write C10200 (OF) or C10100 (OFE). C10100 has tighter oxygen and impurity maxima; do not treat them as interchangeable for vacuum/electronic duty. Decorative facade copper almost never needs C10100. Paying for OFE does not make the hole pattern look sharper.

Don't order without…

Confirming joining method and conductivity requirement in the same email as the grade. Brazed HVAC panel ≠ peak EMI shield. Same word “copper,” wrong grade, wrong job — and the MTC will not save you if the PO never said which UNS.

What perforation does not change

Punching does not convert C11000 into C10200. Alloy chemistry is set at the mill. Perforation changes geometry, residual stress, local cold work, and effective stiffness. Mill test certificates track the base sheet. Do not expect GARG to “certify holes into oxygen-free.”

Grade vs temper

Grade is chemistry. Temper is mechanical condition (O60 soft / H02 half-hard / H04 hard in trade language — codes per ASTM B601). Soft C11000 and hard C11000 are the same grade and different animals on the brake. See copper sheet temper codes.

RFQ language that works

Material: ASTM B152 C11000, temper O60 (soft) Alternate if joining: ASTM B152 C12200, temper H02 (1/2 hard) Do not substitute C10100/C10200 unless approved in writing. Confirm %IACS floor on mill cert if electrical requirement applies.

Oxygen-free duty — name the UNS:

Material: ASTM B152 C10200 (OF), temper as drawing MTC required: chemistry + temper. No C11000 substitution. (If OFE required: C10100 — do not write “OFHC” alone.)

Checklist before you freeze the grade

  • UNS grade written (not only ETP / DHP / OFHC)
  • Joining method: none / solder / braze / weld / furnace — and atmosphere if known
  • Conductivity floor stated if it exists — or explicitly “not critical”
  • Vacuum / reducing atmosphere / high-heat process flagged if any
  • Temper called out separately (ASTM B601 code preferred)
  • MTC requirement stated if purchasing needs it on the dock
