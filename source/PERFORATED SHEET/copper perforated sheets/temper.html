Mechanical condition

Soft vs Hard Temper for Copper Sheet with Holes

Grade is chemistry. Temper is how hard and springy the sheet is. Same C11000 in soft anneal will wrap around a radius. Same C11000 hard will fight you and hold a flat panel better. Spec both with ASTM codes — or GARG INDUSTRIAL MESH has to guess which animal you wanted.

Temper designations for copper follow ASTM B601 (used by ASTM B152). B601 names the code. Property limits for a given temper live in the B152 tables for that alloy and thickness — not in B601 alone. Trade words soft / half-hard / hard map roughly to O60 / H02 / H04, but tensile/hardness windows are alloy- and thickness-specific. Soft words alone are not a complete PO.

Don't order without…

Blank temper before punching (ASTM code preferred). Whether post-punch flatten/anneal is allowed. Whether the panel will be roll-formed, brake-bent, or welded after perforation. Mechanical property tests on perforated product are almost never identical to the blank mill cert — do not assume they are.

ASTM B601 codes you will see on sheet

Cold-worked H-series (rolled)

ASTM code Trade name Perforated use notes (industry practice)
H00 1/8 hard Light cold work. Between soft and quarter-hard.
H01 1/4 hard Modest stiffness; still workable. Common middle ground for panels that need body without spring temper.
H02 1/2 hard (half-hard) Noticeably stiffer. Common sweet spot for flat screens and framed panels.
H03 3/4 hard Between half-hard and hard. Follow the drawing if named.
H04 Hard High strength, low elongation. Springs back. Self-supporting / gasket-seat territory — confirm hole vs thickness.
H06 Extra hard Less common on architectural screens. Drawing-driven.
H08 Spring Spring temper. Confirm before buying blank for perforation.

Annealed O-series (and related)

ASTM code Meaning
O60 Soft annealed — the usual “soft / annealed” callout on sheet

If your drawing names a code, we follow the drawing. If it says only “hard copper” or “soft copper,” we will ask which ASTM code you mean before we buy the blank.

Soft vs half-hard vs hard — punch, form, flatness

The comparison below is shop guidance for perforated copper. It is not a substitute for B152 property tables. Flatness on soft copper with wide margins or high open area should be agreed separately — do not import steel DIN flatness tables as gospel for copper.

Concern Soft (O60) Half-hard (H02) Hard (H04)
Punching Copper is soft and punches more readily than stainless. Kind to tooling. Burrs still happen if die clearance is wrong. Normal production territory for most copper perf jobs. Higher punch load and tooling wear. Small holes relative to thickness need prior technical clarification.
Forming after perf Best for curves, wraps, brake-formed returns. Dense holes can still tear at bridges if you bend too tight. Limited forming. Gentle radii OK. Do not expect soft-temper wrap behaviour. Poor candidate for tight bends. Springback. Crack risk at bridges on dense patterns.
Flatness in service Waves and oil-cans on thin sheet / high OA% / long spans / wide unequal margins. Needs good framing or agreed flatness limits. Usually the practical flat panel choice. Still respect span and OA%. Best chance of staying planar against gaskets and light load — until holes and OA% remove the stiffness you paid for.
Handling / shipping Fingerprints and dents. Paper interleave and crate matter. More forgiving than soft. Stiffer panels; edges still nick. Packing still required.
Spec tip

Trade shortcut: soft ≈ O60, half-hard ≈ H02, hard ≈ H04 — then confirm against B152 for your thickness. For flat framed panels: start the conversation at H01/H02. For brake-formed wraps: O60. For lids that must stay flat against a gasket: H04 — and keep open area and thickness realistic.

What punching does to temper

Perforating cold-works local ligaments around every hole. Residual stress. The as-delivered perforated temper is not identical to the blank temper without agreement. The mill temper on the cert is the starting condition of the blank. Blank edges stay closer to mill condition; the perforated field is a different animal.

Do not assume “H02 on the cert” means “H02 everywhere after a high open-area punch.” If you need soft finished condition after heavy punching and forming, say whether you want soft mill stock going in, or anneal after fabrication. Post-punch anneal/flatten is a separate process decision — confirm on order. Do not assume it is included in a standard punch quote.

Temper vs punchability

Industry practice (not a copper-specific ASTM/DIN law): start at minimum hole diameter ≈ material thickness (ratio ≥ 1:1) and minimum bridge ≈ material thickness. Harder materials often need larger hole/bridge relative to thickness; copper punches more readily than stainless, but 1:1 remains the safe default starting rule. Sub-thickness holes may be possible with suitable tooling — that requires prior technical clarification with GARG, not a universal promise.

Hole vs thickness Soft (O60) H02 / H04
Hole clearly > thickness Routine when tooling exists Routine when tooling exists
Hole ≈ thickness (≈1:1) Usual production floor — confirm dense patterns Confirm; harder temper raises punch load
Hole < thickness Buyer-confirm / process discussion — not a silent catalogue checkbox Higher risk for conventional punch; expect clarification or alternative process

Conductivity note

Cold work lowers conductivity relative to annealed mill data of the same grade. Exact %IACS after perforation is buyer-confirm territory — mill certs are usually blank-based. For most perforated screens, open-area geometry dominates electrical/thermal behaviour more than the temper delta. If a %IACS floor is on the drawing, put it on the RFQ and say whether it applies to the blank or the finished panel. Punching removes metal and adds cold work; it does not improve conductivity.

RFQ language that works

Temper: ASTM B601 H02 (1/2 hard) as mill blank before perforation. Finished use: flat panel in frame — no post-punch anneal unless quoted. Forming after punch: none. Burr side: down (away from show face). Flatness: agree limit if critical (soft Cu — do not assume steel DIN tables).

Or for formed work:

Temper: O60 soft annealed mill blank. Post-punch: brake form long edges — confirm min bend radius vs bridge. No anneal after punch unless required — confirm on quote.
Don't order without…

An ASTM temper code (or clear soft / 1/4 / 1/2 / hard mapped to O60 / H01 / H02 / H04), whether post-punch forming or anneal is in scope, and whether flatness is cosmetic or gasket-critical. “Hard enough” is not a temper.
