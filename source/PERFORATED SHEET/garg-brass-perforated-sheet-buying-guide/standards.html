Standards thinking

ASTM B36 Brass Sheet - What Perforation Adds

Buy the sheet to a metal standard. Buy the holes to a drawing or pattern callout. Mixing those two into one vague sentence is how certificates get messy. At GARG INDUSTRIAL MESH, “ASTM B36” on a perforated brass quote means the base metal blank — not DIN hole-pattern compliance. Do not use copper’s ASTM B152 as the primary callout for brass sheet.

Don't order without…

Separating metal cert requirements from perforation acceptance. MTC for chemistry and temper ≠ first-article hole/pitch check. Name the UNS brass grade — C26000, C27000, C28000, or C23000 as required.

ASTM B36 / B36M — the blank brass sheet standard

ASTM B36 / B36M is the common product specification for brass plate, sheet, strip, and rolled bar made from listed UNS brass alloys (including cartridge brass C26000, yellow brass grades such as C27000, Muntz C28000, red brass C23000, and other compositions in the standard’s scope).

It sets requirements for chemical composition, temper, and mechanical properties for the unperforated mill product. Punching/perforating is secondary manufacturing; mill certificates typically cover the blank, not finished perforated panel geometry.

B36M is the metric companion. Write the units you want on thickness and sheet size. Put the year of issue on the PO when it matters so the mill cert matches the edition you ordered.

Spec tip

Stack the callouts on two lines: Material: ASTM B36 C26000, temper O60 then Perforation: Round 60° (Rv), Ø4.0 / pitch 6.0, blank edge 25 mm. Metal first. Holes second.

Covered by ASTM B36 (base sheet) Not covered — specify separately

Temper and mechanical property windows for sheet Blank edge / margin width
Mill testing / certification language for the brass product Pattern orientation (60° / 90° / DIN Rv–Rg codes)

Brass B36 vs copper B152 — do not mix

Copper sheet for perforation is commonly called out to ASTM B152. Brass sheet is commonly called out to ASTM B36. Writing “ASTM B152 brass” is a documentation error. Writing only “ASTM perforated brass” tells us neither the alloy nor the hole pattern.

Perforation is a process on top of the sheet

  1. Buy / certify the blank to ASTM B36 (UNS grade + temper + thickness).
  2. Punch / perforate to a pattern callout or drawing (hole, pitch, orientation, OA% target, blank edge).
  3. Cut / finish / pack to finished size and site requirements.

There is no single ASTM number that means “this perforated brass panel is approved in full.” Mill B36 cert ≠ perforated dimensional inspection report.

Temper / mechanical properties Ties blank to O60 / H01–H04 windows Property limits live in B36 tables for the temper

DIN 24041 pattern naming — holes, not brass chemistry

European trade still talks in DIN 24041-family abbreviations for perforated plates. Useful shorthand for geometry — independent of material. It is not a substitute for ASTM B36.

Code Meaning GARG catalogue thinking
RvRound holes, 60° staggeredRound 60°
RgRound holes, straight rowsRound 90°
Qg / QvSquare holesSquare 90° common
Hex (trade)Hexagonal openingsHex 60° — send print if non-standard

Example: Rv 5–8 = round staggered, hole Ø 5 mm, pitch 8 mm. Soft brass with wide unequal blank margins or high open area can show larger flatness deviations — agree flatness separately. Citing “DIN 24041” on a brass PO correctly means pattern geometry naming.
