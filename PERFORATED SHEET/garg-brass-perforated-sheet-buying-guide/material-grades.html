Material selection

C26000 Cartridge Brass Grades for Sheets with Holes

If your PO says “brass perforated sheet” and nothing else, you have not specified a metal. You have specified a colour family. Zinc content drives colour, cold formability, corrosion behaviour, cost, and lead time. Spec the UNS grade. Then GARG INDUSTRIAL MESH can buy the right blank and punch it.

For brass plate, sheet, strip, and rolled bar, the usual product standard is ASTM B36 / B36M. It covers chemical composition, temper, and mechanical properties for the unperforated mill product. Punching is secondary manufacturing. Mill certificates typically cover the blank, not finished hole geometry. See ASTM B36 brass sheet standards. Do not cite copper’s ASTM B152 as the primary sheet standard for brass.

Don't order without…

A UNS grade (C26000 / C27000 / C28000 / C23000 — not “yellow brass” or “cartridge brass” alone if colour match is contractual), plus one sentence on end use: decorative screen / elevator panel / speaker grille / marine accent / furniture. End use steers alloy faster than a brochure photo.

Why “just brass” is wrong

Commercial brass is a family of copper–zinc alloys. Copper percentage changes colour (red → yellow → golden), dezincification risk in some waters, and how readily the sheet cold-forms before and after perforation. Write the UNS number.

  • C26000 (cartridge brass, ~70/30) — classic yellow-gold. Excellent cold formability. Default for most perforated brass sheet in architectural and industrial decorative work.
  • C27000 (yellow brass, ~65/35) — slightly more zinc; similar yellow family. Common general-purpose sheet callout — confirm availability and colour match to sample.
  • C28000 (Muntz metal, ~60/40) — more golden / reddish-yellow. Often preferred for architectural accents and some marine decorative work; typically stronger hot-working story than deep cold draw.
  • C23000 (red brass, ~85/15) — reddish tone closer to copper. Better atmospheric corrosion behaviour in many architectural contexts; used when the design wants a warmer red brass panel with holes.

Spec tip

Write both alloy and product standard: ASTM B36 C26000. Prefer UNS numbers over “cartridge,” “yellow,” or “Muntz” alone when colour matching a previous lot. If you only have a local trade name, ask GARG to confirm the nearest UNS equivalent before production.

Corrosion & colour reality

Brass tarnishes. High-zinc alloys can be more susceptible to dezincification in aggressive waters — relevant for some marine / wet environments even when the panel is “decorative.” Indoor elevator and furniture duty is usually about fingerprints, lacquer, and cleaning chemistry — not ocean water. Outdoor architectural cladding needs a finish plan (clear lacquer, oil, or accepted patina) written on the RFQ.

What perforation does not change

Punching does not convert C26000 into C23000. Alloy chemistry is set at the mill. Perforation changes geometry, residual stress, local cold work, and effective stiffness. Mill test certificates track the base sheet.

Alloy vs temper

Alloy is chemistry. Temper is mechanical condition (annealed / 1/4 hard / 1/2 hard / hard — ASTM B601 codes such as O60 / H01 / H02 / H04). Soft C26000 and hard C26000 are the same alloy and different animals on the brake. See brass sheet temper codes.

RFQ language that works

Material: ASTM B36 C26000 (cartridge brass), temper O60 (annealed) Alternate if yellow brass named: ASTM B36 C27000, temper H02 (1/2 hard) Do not substitute C23000/C28000 unless approved in writing (colour).

Checklist before you freeze the alloy

  • UNS grade written (not only “yellow brass” / “cartridge”)
  • Colour sample approval if matching existing cabin / facade metal
  • Indoor vs outdoor / marine exposure flagged
  • Finish / lacquer stated
  • Temper called out separately
  • MTC requirement stated if purchasing needs it on the dock
