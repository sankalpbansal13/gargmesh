Unperforated borders

Blank Margin and Unperforated Border on Copper Sheet

The blank edge is the unperforated strip around (or along) the sheet — where you clamp, weld, hem, gasket, and hide fasteners. We customize 0–100 mm on our floor every day. Beyond 100 mm is available on request. Spec the millimetres. Do not leave margin to “whatever the press leaves.”

What a blank edge is

Also called a margin or unperforated border. Pattern stops inside this strip. Finished cut size should include the blank edge — say so if your drawing shows outer overall vs perforated field only. Incomplete (breakout) holes at a trim line are normal when the margin is small relative to pitch; tell us whether that is acceptable on your RFQ.

Spec tip

Write margins per side if they differ: LE 15 / RE 15 / TE 40 / BE 40 mm (or a sketch). “20 mm border” means all four sides equal unless you say otherwise.

Why margins matter

Concern What the blank edge does
Mounting / framing Gives a solid land for screws, welds, hems, and clamps — not a hole row.
Strength Perforated fields are weaker; borders carry edge loads and reduce tear-out at fasteners.
Weight Borders are solid copper. Wide margins raise shipping weight even when field OA% is high.
Sealing / EMI Continuous contact land for gaskets on lids and vent panels.
Appearance Clean frame line around a decorative field.

Side margins vs end margins

On cut-to-size sheets, specify all four sides. On coil-fed or long-sheet work, “side” margins (along the length) and “end” margins (across the width) can be controlled differently depending on our setup. For coiled stock you will shear later, say which edges must stay blank after cutting. Unequal margins are common on architectural panels (wider bottom for kick or fixing) — send us the sketch early so we plan punch path and leveling on our floor.

No-margin vs safe-margin jobs

Approach When it fits What to expect
No-margin (0 mm) Full-field look; decorative wrap; max perforated area Edge hole breakout common on our runs. Weaker edges. Framing must not rely on a solid strip.
Minimal / tight margin Trimmed panels where a slim border is enough Still state the mm. Incomplete edge holes may appear if margin < ~½ pitch.
Safe margin Weld-in frames, gasket lids, structural fastening Sized to fastener edge distance, gasket width, or weld land. Often 15–40 mm to start; then match the frame drawing.

Safe-margin is not a catalogue SKU — it is “enough solid metal for your joint.” Your frame or gasket drawing wins.

Don't order without…

Stating whether finished cut size includes the blank edge (it should). Also whether incomplete edge holes are acceptable when the pattern runs near a trim line.

Margins vs open area accounting

Catalogue OA% is for the perforated field. A 1000×2000 panel with 50 mm borders all around has a smaller perforated rectangle inside. Airflow and EMI aperture area follow the perforated zone. Weight must include solid borders. If your consultant calculated OA% on the full outer rectangle, fix the calc or fix the margins — one of them is wrong.

Practical starting points (guidance only)

Situation Starting margin talk
General framed panel 15–40 mm all around — then adjust to frame section
Weld-in filter element Sized to weld land; often 10–30 mm
EMI lid with gasket Continuous land matching gasket width + tolerance
Decorative full-field look Small margin or 0 mm — accept edge hole breakout
Structural edge fastening Margin ≥ fastener edge distance required by your design
