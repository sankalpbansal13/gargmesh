Thickness vs purpose

Copper Mesh Sheet Thickness by Application

Thickness is money, stiffness, and punchability. Too thin and the panel oil-cans. Too thick and your small holes stop being punchable. The bands below are shop guidance — not building-code law. Confirm against span, load, temper, and hole size before you freeze the drawing.

Hard rule of thumb first

For conventional punching: hole diameter (or minimum hole opening) ≥ material thickness. That is the production-friendly floor. Soft copper can sometimes go tighter; hard temper and dense patterns push you back toward (or above) 1:1. Below 1:1 is a process discussion with GARG — not a silent assumption on the RFQ.

Don't order without…

Checking hole size against thickness before the aesthetic render leaves the office. A 1.0 mm hole in 2.0 mm copper is a manufacturing problem, not a catalogue checkbox.

Guidance bands by application

These ranges show where most jobs land. Your drawing wins. Custom thicknesses are available on order when the mill blank and punch route allow.

Application Typical thickness band (guidance) What drives the number Confirm on order
Architecture / facade / decorative screens ~0.5–1.5 mm common; heavier for long spans or abuse Visual flatness, wind load, framing, patina handling Span, fixings, cladding-only vs structural duty
EMI / RFI shielding panels ~0.2–1.0 mm often used; thicker for lids that must stay flat Weight, formability, gasket contact, aperture vs frequency Frequency intent, contact scheme — we do not invent SE numbers
Filtration / sieving / strainers ~0.3–2.0 mm typical; hole size usually leads Particle cut size, differential pressure, cleanability Hole tolerance, burr side (flow direction), media compatibility
HVAC / thermal / airflow ~0.5–2.0 mm depending on duct or panel duty Open area, stiffness, joining (often points to C12200) OA% on perforated field vs full panel; braze/solder plan
Heavy process / industrial guards ~1.5–3.0+ mm when impact or span demands it Impact, span, fastener edge distance Hole ≥ thickness still applies; prefer larger holes in thick plate
Spec tip

State thickness in millimetres as a decimal — not only “gauge.” Gauge tables differ by material family. 1.2 mm is unambiguous. “18 ga copper” invites conversion arguments on the dock.

Thickness vs open area vs stiffness

Raise open area and the panel softens fast. Raise thickness and you buy stiffness back — until holes get smaller than the sheet is thick. For long architectural spans, many jobs win with: moderate OA%, slightly heavier sheet, sensible blank edges for framing, and a harder temper. Extreme OA% in soft thin copper looks sharp in a render and waves in the sun.

Custom thicknesses on order

GARG punches to the blank you specify when tooling and hole size allow. Non-catalogue thicknesses are normal — call out the exact mm (or inch) on the RFQ, plus grade and temper. Ultra-thin foil and holes smaller than thickness may need a specialty route. That is quote territory, not a silent catalogue pick.

Weight (quick reality check)

Solid copper density is about 8.9 g/cm³. Perforated weight ≈ solid weight × (1 − OA%/100) over the perforated field, then add the solid blank-edge strips. Use OA% of the perforated zone when you estimate shipping weight — not a marketing round number.
